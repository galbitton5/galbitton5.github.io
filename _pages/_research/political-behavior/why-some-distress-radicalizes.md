  <p class="project-title">“When Does War Exposure Become Political Violence? Distress, Cognitive Flexibility, and Domestic Violent Intentions.” With Daphna Canetti, Sivan Hirsch-Hoefler, and Tal Shaanan. (<em>Under Review</em>) </p>
  <p class="project-links">
    <a class="toggle-abstract">▶ Abstract</a>
  </p>
  <div class="abstract">
    <p>When does exposure to war foster domestic political violence? We distinguish between endorsing violence against the government and personally being willing to act against elected officials. We argue that these two political responses follow different psychological pathways: exposure may be associated directly with endorsement, whereas the intention to use political violence is linked to an affective pathway through psychological distress. We test this argument with a two-wave panel fielded in the immediate aftermath of the June 2025 Israel--Iran war and during a later period of relative de-escalation. Greater exposure predicted higher distress at follow-up, conditional on immediate-postwar distress, which in turn predicted greater intention but not greater endorsement. This indirect pathway was concentrated among respondents lower in cognitive flexibility and was not explained by heightened threat perception. The findings suggest that the political consequences of war exposure are heterogeneous: the same exposure reaches different forms of domestic political violence through different psychological pathways. </p>
