Methodological Innovations
----
<div class="project">
  <p class="project-title">“Are findings of research on the Effectiveness of International Economic Organizations generalizable?” With Tal Sadeh and Bernhard Reinsberg. </p>
  <p class="project-links">
    <a class="toggle-abstract">▶ Abstract</a>
  </p>
  <div class="abstract">
    <p>The literature on the effectiveness of International Economic Organizations (IEO) assistance programs has in recent decades mostly relied on quantitative evidence to substantiate its claims; this trend can be expected to continue given the increasing wealth of data sources and methods. Large-N datasets are supposed to improve the generalizability of findings, but few studies if any probe the generalizability of their results, in part for lack of proper metrics. We begin to fill this gap by developing indicators of the generalizability of estimates in regression analysis, specifically those typical of IEO studies, to the entire nominal sample (internal validity), and to a target population (external validity). We use these indicators to study the literature on the effectiveness of IMF programs, a sub-field of the economic IEO research agenda, replicating results from 677 models in 43 related articles in peer-reviewed journals (all such articles for which we could obtain replication files). We find that many estimates across different studies are effectively based on a small set of countries or years, even when their nominal samples are large. We demonstrate that economic IEO scholars must trade-off high-precision treatment operationalization, model specification and careful causal identification against generalizability of their findings. Our indicators can help scholars manage and optimize this trade-off. </p>
