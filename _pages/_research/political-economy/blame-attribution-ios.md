Banking, Blame, and Democratic Accountability
----
<div class="project">

  <p class="project-links">

    <a class="toggle-abstract">▶ Abstract</a>
  </p>
  <div class="abstract">
    <p>Does it matter to citizens whether responsibility for a policy rests with a national government or an international organization? Research on international authority reaches competing conclusions: citizens may respond to the location of authority, or they may care primarily about policy outcomes. We connect this debate to electoral accountability by testing whether information about the allocation of supervisory authority changes the penalty citizens impose on governing parties after a policy failure. We use a preregistered paired-profile conjoint experiment with 1,724 participants in Germany. The experiment independently randomizes the allocation of authority over bank supervision and whether a bank rescue is funded by taxpayers. Under national supervision, a taxpayer-funded rescue reduces stated support for governing-party profiles by 19.8 percentage points. When the EU is also involved in supervision, that penalty is 13.6 points smaller, although governing-party profiles still incur a net penalty. The difference is larger among Eurosceptic respondents but is not confined to them. These findings show that the institutional location of authority can moderate retrospective evaluations of national incumbents.</p>
